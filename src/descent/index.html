    How this is measured

    What the number is
    Every step measures the same thing: mutual information between one coda and the coda that follows it, in bits. Zero bits means knowing one coda tells you nothing about the next. A pair is only ever counted inside a block, so no pair straddles two recording sessions.
    What is subtracted from it
    A finite sample produces apparent structure even when there is none, so no raw value appears on this page on its own. Each step builds 25 matched null models by shuffling the real symbols inside their own blocks, which keeps whatever the step controls for and destroys only the order. The reported number is the real measurement minus the average of those nulls, and the error bar is how far the nulls themselves wander.
    What the coverage flag means
    A step is flagged when it has fewer than five observed pairs for each cell of the table of possible neighbour pairs. The rule is deliberately strict, and it is applied to the last step even though that step's result is far above its null. Both facts are shown together rather than the flattering one alone.
    Where the numbers come from
    Nothing here is stored. The corpus loads into your browser and the whole ladder is computed on the spot by the same tested module the project runs offline, with a fixed random seed, so the page and the command line produce the same ladder. The offline report shows an extra decimal place, which is why the figures there look longer than the ones here.
